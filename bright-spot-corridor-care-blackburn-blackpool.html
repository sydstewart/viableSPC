Blackburn and Blackpool — Improving Flow Across Hospital and Social Care

The reported improvements include fewer long A&E waits and a cleared corridor at Blackburn. The learning question is how coordination across hospital and community services contributed—and whether the gains persist.

What was reported

The original case account reports that Royal Blackburn Hospital cleared its main corridor in April 2026 and reduced 12-hour A&E waits by 18% during 2025/26. Blackpool Teaching Hospitals reported a 42% reduction in 12-hour waits, alongside shorter stays and fewer discharge delays.

These are different outcomes: clearing one corridor is not the same as eliminating corridor care across a hospital, and fewer long waits do not alone establish safer care. The results justify investigation without making the two trusts interchangeable.

What changed in practice

Getting It Right First Time (GIRFT) teams supported the improvement work. Two approaches feature in the account:

  • Transfer of Care Hubs: bringing hospital discharge staff and social-care colleagues together to coordinate decisions and resolve delays. Co-location may help information travel, but does not automatically align budgets or decision rights.
  • Discharge to Assess: assessing appropriate patients’ longer-term care needs outside an acute bed, with the necessary support. This depends on safe planning, clinical judgement and sufficient community services—not simply moving assessment elsewhere.

The practical change is to make responsibility for the next step clearer across organisational boundaries, rather than leaving patients waiting while separate teams exchange requests.

Why this matters

Hospital flow can depend on decisions and capacity outside a trust’s direct control. A useful starting point is to follow a delayed discharge: what is missing, who can provide it and who has authority to decide?

Shared visibility can support cooperation; it cannot create a care package, workforce or joint financial agreement by itself. The transferable mechanism is timely, coordinated action with the resources to carry it out—not a hub room or meeting format alone. See Joiner’s Levels of Fix for the distinction between process and system changes.

What remains unproven

The case describes GIRFT comparing Blackpool with more than 100 peer trusts. A different trajectory is more informative than a before-and-after percentage alone, but peer comparison does not automatically control for case mix, starting position, local capacity or other interventions. It is not proof of causation.

  • Persistence: do gains hold after specialist support or monitoring reduces?
  • Dependencies: are partner staffing, decision rights and community capacity maintained?
  • Safety: what happened to readmissions, incidents, patient experience and workload in community services?

A pre-committed Bootstrap CUSUM evaluation is not provided in the account. Suitable time-series and comparative analysis could strengthen evaluation, but a change point cannot identify which intervention caused the result.

How to take this further

  • Bright Spot Analysis — investigate the mechanism and test transferability.
  • Corridor Care 2029 — connect local learning with evaluation of wider programmes.
  • PSIRF and Systemic Learning — connect patient-safety concerns, responses and evaluation.
