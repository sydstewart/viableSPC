Fishbone (Ishikawa) Diagrams: From Hypothesis to Evidence

A diagram identifies potential causes—but it doesn't prove them. Use this structured approach to turn your brainstorming session into a testable investigation.

The "Diagram Trap"

Most fishbone diagrams are used as a one-off brainstorming exercise and then abandoned. To prevent recurrence, your diagram must lead to a specific, pre-specified test of change.

How to use the diagram

  1. Define the effect: Place your problem statement clearly at the head.
  2. Categorize: Use the 6Ms (or custom clinical equivalents) to map potential causes[cite: 1].
  3. Verify: For every branch, ask: "Is this an active system failure or a theoretical hypothesis?"[cite: 1]
📈 Validate your findings

A diagram generates a hypothesis, but data confirms it. Before acting on your fishbone branches, use our StepChange Analyzer to check your event rate for a structural change point[cite: 1].
